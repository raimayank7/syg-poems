Boliyan

Punjabi words in boliyan and what they mean

Boliyan come from village life, and they are full of words that a city child, or a Punjabi family settled far from Punjab, may never have heard at home: the banera where the crow sits, the trinjan where girls once spun together, the nanke who arrive with the wedding gifts. A boli can be sung without knowing them, but it cannot be felt. This page explains the words that return again and again, in four groups.

Each word is given in Gurmukhi and in Roman letters, with a plain explanation. The Roman spelling is only a reading aid; the same word is written in several ways in English, so "veer", "vir" and "veera" are all the same brother. Meanings are given as they are used in folk songs, and a word may be used a little differently in different parts of Punjab.

Family and relations

ਦਾਦਕੇdaadkeThe father's family.

ਸਹੁਰੇsahureA married woman's in-laws' home.

The wedding

ਨਾਨਕੀ ਸ਼ੱਕnaanki shakkThe gifts brought to a wedding by the mother's family.

ਸ਼ਗਨshaganA gift or token given for good luck on a happy occasion.
ਡੋਲੀdoliThe palanquin in which a bride once left her parents' home; now, the moment of her leaving.
ਵਧਾਈਆਂvadhaiyanCongratulations.

Home, village and work

WordRomanMeaning
ਵਿਹੜਾvehdaThe courtyard of a house, where much of family life and most giddha takes place.
ਬਨੇਰਾbaneraThe low wall around a flat roof. Crows sitting on it are said to announce guests.
ਚਰਖਾcharkhaThe spinning wheel.
ਤ੍ਰਿੰਞਣtrinjanA gathering of girls and women spinning together, where many old songs were made.
ਫੁਲਕਾਰੀphulkariA shawl embroidered with flower patterns; literally "flower work".
ਮਧਾਣੀ, ਚਾਟੀmadhaani, chaatiThe churning stick, and the earthen pot in which curd is churned for butter.
ਗਾਗਰgaagarA metal water pot. A decorated one is carried in the jaago.
ਪੀਂਘpeenghA swing, hung from a tree in the rainy season.
ਸਾਉਣsaun (sawan)The rainy month of the Punjabi calendar, and the season of Teeyan.
ਖੱਟਣkhattanTo earn. "Khattan gaya si" means "he had gone away to earn".

Giddha and singing

WordRomanMeaning
ਬੋਲੀ ਪਾਉਣੀboli pauniTo "put" a boli: to lead one in the circle.
ਤੋੜਾtoda (tod)The closing line of a boli, which the whole circle repeats.
ਹੇਕhekA long, drawn-out note with which some songs begin.
ਤਾੜੀtaadiA clap. The clap is the heartbeat of the giddha.
ਢੋਲਕੀdholkiThe small two-sided drum played at women's singing evenings.
ਰੌਣਕraunakLiveliness; the happy noise of a full house.
ਸ਼ਾਵਾ, ਬੱਲੇ ਬੱਲੇshaava, balle balleShouts of praise and joy, like "bravo".
ਨੀ, ਵੇni, veLittle calling words. "Ni" is used when speaking to a woman or girl, "ve" to a man or boy.
ਕੁੜੇ, ਬੀਬਾkude, beebaAffectionate ways of addressing a girl, or a dear one of any age.

Why family words matter so much

English has one word, "aunt", where Punjabi has maasi, maami, bhua, chaachi and taayi, and each of them plays a different part at a wedding. Boliyan are built on these differences. A verse addressed to the maama is about the mother's family and its duties of honour. A verse about the nanaan and the bhabi is about two women, a sister and a brother's wife, who share a house. When you know who is who, a boli that seemed to be a plain rhyme turns into a small scene from family life. If you are making your own boli, choosing the exact relation is the quickest way to make it sound real.

Words that mean more than they say

Some words carry a feeling beyond their dictionary meaning. "Peke" is not simply the parents' house; for a married daughter in a folk song it is childhood, ease and belonging. "Sawan" is not only a month; it is the swings, the rain and a visit home. "Chann", the moon, is the beloved, and a mother also uses it for her child. Listen for these when you read the verses on this site, and the English meanings we give under each boli will open up further.

Using this page

Keep it open while you read the giddha boliyan, the wedding boliyan or the tappe. If you are teaching children, pick five words, act each one out, and then sing a boli that uses them. For how the verses are performed, read how to put a boli in the giddha.
