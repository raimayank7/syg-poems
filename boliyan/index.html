    Old verses and new ones

    Punjab's boliyan were never written by one person. They were made up at spinning sessions, weddings and fairs, and passed on by singing. This site keeps the two kinds apart: 21 traditional verses, given as they are commonly sung, and 92 new verses written for this site in the same patterns, about brothers and bhabis, grandmothers, school farewells and even the family group chat. Each verse says which kind it is.

    If you are learning, start with what a boli is, then read how to put a boli in the giddha. Words such as nanke, banera and phulkari are explained in the glossary of words in boliyan.

    Questions people ask

    What is a boli?

    A boli is a short Punjabi folk verse that is sung during the giddha or bhangra. One person sings the lines, and everyone joins in on the last line with claps and dancing. The plural is boliyan.

    Are these boliyan traditional?

    Each verse is labelled. "Traditional" verses are old folk verses with no known author, given as they are commonly sung. "New" verses were written for this site in the traditional patterns and are clearly marked.

    I cannot read Gurmukhi. Can I still use them?

    Yes. Every verse is also written in Roman (English) letters, with its meaning in English. You can copy either script.

    Can I use these boliyan at a wedding or a school function?

    Yes, freely. Traditional verses belong to everybody, and you may sing the new ones at any family, school or community event. All of them are clean and safe for a mixed gathering.
