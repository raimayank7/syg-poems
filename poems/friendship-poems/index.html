Poems on friendship

Poems for the friend who has seen you at your silliest. Send one on Friendship Day, on a birthday, or on an ordinary Tuesday when a memory turns up.

English

  • The Back Bench

    We sat where teachers rarely looked, the last row, by the broken fan. You drew moustaches in my book, I ate your lunch. So it began. The bell has rung a thousand times in schools we never see. But somewhere there's a wooden bench still saving seats for you and me.

  • A Friend Is

    A friend is tea at half past four, a knock you know upon the door, a "tell me" when you cannot speak, a "told you so" kept for next week. A friend is change for the bus fare, a borrowed pen, a saved-up chair, the one who laughs before the joke, and stays to listen when you choke.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Long Distance

    You moved away. The map got wide. The group went quiet on the phone. We like each other's photographs and call it staying known. But send one line, a single word, the old bad joke about the bus, and ten years fold up like a chair. There we are. It's us.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Two Cups

    Two cups of tea, one plate of fries, a table by the door. We solve the country, then our lives, then order just one more. The waiter knows us. So does rain. The bill is always fought. Some friendships live in grand events. Ours lives where tea is brought.

    ShareYourGroup

  • The Group Chat

    Forty messages by eight, A meme, a plan, a fight. Somebody's baby learnt to walk, Somebody cannot sleep tonight. We live in seven different towns And one small glowing square. I mute it twice a week, it's true. I am glad that it is there.

  • New Friend

    You sat beside me, that was all, The day I knew no one. You shared your lunch and half your bench, And something had begun. I have forgotten what we ate And what the lesson was. I still remember being new, And not alone, because.

When to send a friendship poem

Friendship Day is on the first Sunday of August in India, and a poem is a good change from a forwarded picture. Birthdays, farewells and reunions are natural moments too. The best time, though, is for no reason: a poem that arrives on an ordinary afternoon says "I thought of you", which is the whole point of friendship.

Make it yours

Friendship lives in details that nobody else would understand, so add one. The name of the canteen. The teacher you both feared. The scooter that never started. The city one of you moved to. In "The Back Bench", change the bench to wherever the two of you actually sat. In "Long Distance", put in the real time difference. If you are sending the Hindi or Punjabi poem, use the name you call your friend, which is probably not the name on their certificates.

Ways to use these poems

  • Handwritten on the first page of a slam book or a farewell diary.
  • Read aloud at a friend's birthday or sangeet, with the last line changed to a private joke.
  • As the caption of an old photograph of the two of you.
  • In a group chat on the anniversary of the day the group was made.

What to avoid

Do not send a friendship poem to make up after a quarrel without also saying sorry in plain words. A poem can open the door, but the apology has to walk through it. And if a friend is going through something hard, choose a quiet poem such as "Two Cups" over a cheerful one.

Good to know

  • Change one line to a memory only the two of you share. It becomes your poem.
