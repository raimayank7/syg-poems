Short poems on life

Poems for the thoughtful hours: a birthday that made you count, a train journey, the end of a school year, an evening on the terrace. They are short, plain and written to be understood on the first reading.

English

  • The Shoes

    I bought him shoes a size too big, "He'll grow into them soon." He wore them slapping down the stairs One sunny afternoon. Today I found them by the door, Too small, the laces thin. Nobody told me when it was He stopped just fitting in.

  • Ordinary Day

    Nothing happened. Tea was made. The paper came at eight. The milkman rang, the pressure cooker Whistled for the plate. One day I shall look back and see, Among the days gone past, That this was happiness itself, So quiet as it passed.

    ShareYourGroup

  • The Clock

    The clock upon my father's wall Has never told a lie. It ticked when I was learning words, It ticks as years go by. It does not hurry, does not wait, For sorrow or for fun. I wind it every Sunday now, The way that he had done.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Platform

    The train is late, the tea is hot, The bench is cold and long. A child is sleeping on a trunk, A porter hums a song. We all are going somewhere else, We all have somewhere been. For twenty minutes, strangers share One board, one clock, one scene.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Old Diary

    I found a diary from the year That I was seventeen. The handwriting was mine, all right. The worries were so green. The exam I thought would end my life, The friend I thought I'd lost. I wish that I could write to him: It's fine. Hold on. It passed.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Enough

    A roof, a meal, a friend to call, A book beside the bed, Work to do with these two hands, A pillow for my head. I counted what I do not have For years, and it was rough. I counted what I have today. It came to more than enough.

What a poem on life is for

Nobody needs a poem to be told that time passes. We need one to notice it. A good short poem takes something everyone knows, that children grow, that parents age and that ordinary days are the ones we later miss, and makes it felt for a moment. These poems do that with everyday things: a growing child's shoes, an old diary, a clock, a station platform.

Where to use them

  • Morning assembly: one poem read slowly, followed by a moment of quiet. "The Shoes" and "आज" work well.
  • Birthdays and farewells: as the thoughtful part of a speech, after the jokes.
  • School magazines: beside an essay on growing up or on time.
  • A message to an old friend or a grown child: with one line of your own added.
  • For yourself: copied into a diary on the last day of the year.

Reading a reflective poem aloud

Slow down more than feels natural. A poem about time needs time. Pause after the line that turns, which is usually the third line of the last stanza. Keep your voice level. These poems do not need sadness added: if the words are right, a plain voice carries the feeling better than a trembling one.

Write one of your own

Take an object you have owned for a long time: a watch, a school bag, a steel glass, a cycle. Write four lines about what it was when new, and four about what it is now. Do not mention time at all. The poem will be about time anyway. More methods are in How to write a poem.

Good to know

  • A poem on life is best sent alone. Do not attach a sermon.

Questions

Are these poems suitable for school assembly?

Yes. They are short, clean and take under a minute to read aloud.
