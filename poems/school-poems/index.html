Poems on teachers, school and farewell

Poems for the stage: Teachers' Day, the farewell function, the annual day. They are short and rhythmic, and easy to learn in one evening.

English

  • To My Teacher

    You taught me where the comma goes, and where the river meets the sea, and that a question, asked aloud, is braver than a guess can be. You marked my errors small, in red, and wrote "well tried" in letters tall. I did not know, at twelve years old, that this was the lesson, after all.

  • The First Bell

    New shoes that bite, a bag too wide, a bottle hanging at my side, a gate as tall as any king, and somewhere, bells begin to ring. My mother's hand slips out of mine. I join the smallest, wobbliest line. I do not cry. Well, not a lot. By lunch I've made a friend. Forgot.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Farewell, Class

    We came in shorts and leave in ties, with longer legs and wiser eyes. The desks we carved, the walls we knew will learn the names of someone new. So thank you, corridors and bell, and teachers who have borne us well. We take the lessons, leave the chairs. The school stays here. We take the stairs.

    ShareYourGroup

  • The Blackboard

    It holds a sum, a map, a rhyme, then loses them, one wipe at a time. But what it lost has gone to stay in forty heads that walked away. So thank the board, so dark and plain: it learns each thing, forgets again, and stands there every morning, clear, as if to say: begin from here.

    ShareYourGroup

  • The Last Period

    The fan still turns, the chalk is short, The bell is five minutes late. She gives us one more sum to do And smiles as we all wait. Tomorrow someone else will stand Where thirty years she stood. We did not always learn the sums. We learnt that she was good.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Homework

    I did it, Ma'am, I promise you, I did it, every bit. I only cannot find the page, The bag, the book, or it. My dog is innocent this time, My brother has not seen. Tomorrow I shall bring it, Ma'am, And two, to show I'm keen.

Which poem for which function

  • Teachers' Day (5 September): "To My Teacher", "The Blackboard" or "गुरु". For a class presentation, let four students take a stanza each.
  • Farewell of a class: "Farewell, Class" or "विदाई", read by a junior to the outgoing seniors, or by a senior in reply.
  • Farewell or retirement of a teacher: "The Last Period" or "To My Teacher", with the teacher's subject worked into a line.
  • The first day of the session: "The First Bell" or "स्कूल के दिन".
  • Alumni reunions: "स्कूल के दिन" and "The Blackboard" bring the old classroom back.

Make it about your own teacher

A poem addressed to "my teacher" becomes a gift when it carries one true detail: the red pen, the chalk dust on a sleeve, the phrase the teacher always says, the way the whole class goes quiet when footsteps come down the corridor. Change one line to hold it. If the poem is for a card, write the teacher's name at the top and sign it with your class and roll number, the way they know you.

Reciting on a school stage

These poems have a steady beat on purpose, so that a nervous speaker can lean on it. Say the title and "a poem for our teachers" before you begin. Speak to the last row. Pause for a breath between stanzas. If the poem ends on thanks, look at the teachers as you say the final line, then say "thank you" once and leave. More help is in our guide, How to recite a poem.

A note for farewell speeches

A four line poem at the end of a farewell speech is stronger than a long poem in the middle. Give your thanks in your own words first, then close with one stanza. People remember the last thing they hear.

Good to know

  • On stage, look up at the end of every second line. It slows you down and lets the hall hear you.
