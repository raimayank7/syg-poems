New rhymes and poems for children

New rhymes for small children, written by us. Each is four to eight lines, with a strong beat, so a child can learn it in a day and recite it with actions.

  • The Lazy Cat

    The cat sat on the garden wall and did not chase the bird at all. "Too hot," she said, "to run today." The bird said, "Thank you," and flew away.

For parents and teachers: how to teach a rhyme

  • Say the whole rhyme once with actions, for fun, before asking the child to repeat anything.
  • Teach two lines at a time. Say them, then say them together, then let the child say them alone.
  • Give every line an action: point at the sky for the sun, wriggle fingers for rain, pat the tummy for samosas.
  • Repeat at odd moments: on the way to school, at bath time, while waiting for the bus. Five short turns beat one long sitting.
  • Praise the attempt. A forgotten line is not a mistake at this age. It is a pause.

Choosing a rhyme for a competition

For nursery and kindergarten, four to eight lines are plenty, and the judges are looking for a clear voice, a smile and simple actions. Choose a rhyme about something the child loves, whether cats, rain, food or the school bag, because real delight shows. Avoid rhymes with words the child cannot pronounce yet. A simple costume or one prop, such as a paper sun or an umbrella, helps a shy child feel like a performer.

Why new rhymes?

Every class knows the old favourites, and at a competition five children may recite the same one. These rhymes were written for this site, so your child's will be the only one of its kind on the stage. They are about things an Indian child sees every day: samosas, rotis, the school bag, ants in a line, the first rain. The complete old classics, such as all five verses of "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star", are on our children's classics page.

Rhymes in Hindi and Punjabi

Small children learn rhymes fastest in the language spoken at home. The Hindi and Punjabi rhymes here use the simplest words, with a beat a child can clap. For a bilingual school function, one child reciting an English rhyme and another its Hindi neighbour, "The Ant" and "चींटी रानी" for example, makes a charming pair.

Good to know

  • Add an action to every line: point, clap, jump, yawn. Children remember with their hands.
  • For a recitation competition in nursery or Class 1, eight lines are plenty.
