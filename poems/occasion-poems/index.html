Poems for birthdays and festivals

A four or eight line poem makes a card feel made by hand. These are for birthdays, anniversaries and festivals. Add the name at the top and yours at the bottom.

English

  • Birthday Verse

    Another candle, one more year, a little wiser, still as dear. May all the things you hope for most arrive on time, like morning toast.

    ShareYourGroup

  • For a Friend's Birthday

    I could have bought a costly gift with ribbon, card and bow. Instead I bring these crooked lines and all the years I owe: the lunches shared, the secrets kept, the calls at half past two. The gift is small. The thanks is not. Happy birthday, you.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Anniversary

    Two toothbrushes in a cup, two cups of tea at dawn, one blanket fought for every night, one life to lean upon. The years have greyed a hair or two and softened every fight. You chose each other long ago. You're choosing still. Tonight.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Diwali Night

    A row of lamps along the wall, a rangoli beside the door, the smell of ghee, a cousin's call, new clothes, and sweets, and then some more. But best of all the lights we light is one no shop has ever sold: the whole house home on Diwali night, the young, the middle and the old.

    ShareYourGroup

  • New Year

    The calendar is new and clean. The page says January one. I do not promise to be great. I promise that I'll get things done: one walk, one book, one kinder word, one phone call home each week. Small things, repeated through the year, will be the change I seek.

    ShareYourGroup

  • For a Parent's Birthday

    You counted all my birthdays first, The candles and the years. You kept the cards I made in school, The drawings and the tears. Today the cake is yours to cut. I cannot count as well. I only know each year you've lived Is one more I can tell.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Rakhi

    A thread of red, a grain of rice, A sweet I did not share. You tied it tight around my wrist, And tied me to your care. We fought that evening, as we do. The thread stayed on all week. Some promises are made out loud. The best ones do not speak.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Housewarming

    Four walls are just four walls, they say, Until the tea is made, Until a child has marked the door And shoes are overlaid. May this house fill with noise and guests And rain upon the pane. May everyone who leaves this door Be glad to come again.

A poem in a card

Printed cards say the same thing to everyone. Four lines in your own handwriting say it to one person. Copy the poem on the left side of the card or on a separate slip, write the person's name above it, and sign below. Leave space around the lines. If your handwriting is poor, write slowly in capital letters or print the poem and add one handwritten sentence underneath. The handwritten sentence is the part they will keep.

Make it personal in one line

  • Birthdays: put the age or the year into the verse, or swap one line for a memory of the year gone by.
  • Anniversaries: name the number of years, and one habit the couple is known for.
  • Diwali, Rakhi, Lohri: mention the home where the family gathers, or the person who will be missed this year.
  • New Year: add one hope that is particular to the person, such as the exam, the new house or the baby.

Reading it at the gathering

At a birthday or anniversary dinner, a short poem read before the cake is cut gives the evening a centre. Stand up, say whom it is for, read slowly, and raise your glass or your hands at the end. Eight lines are the most a party will listen to. Children can do this very well, and a grandchild's reading is usually the high point of a grandparent's birthday.

Sending it on the phone

Use the Copy or WhatsApp button, and add the person's name before the first line. Send it as a personal message, not in a large group, and early in the day. A poem that arrives before the flood of forwards is the one that gets read. For plain wishes without verse, see Wishes.

Good to know

  • In a card, a four line poem is enough. Leave white space around it.
