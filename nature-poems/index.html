Poems on rain, trees and nature

Poems to go with the first rain, a plantation drive or a quiet morning. Useful for Environment Day programmes and for anyone who likes a window seat.

English

  • First Rain

    The sky has kept its promise late. The dust jumps up to meet the drops. A paper boat sets out to sea along the lane, beside the shops. The tin roof plays a thousand drums, the neem tree shakes her hair out, wet. We run outside with open mouths to catch what June has not said yet.

  • The Old Neem

    It asked for nothing: sun, some rain, a little room beside the lane. It gave us shade, a swing, a broom, green twigs for teeth, and nesting room. The road grew wide. The tree grew thin. They measured it. I held my breath. We tied a thread around its trunk, the whole lane, till the man said yes.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Morning

    The milkman's bell, the pressure cooker's sigh, a sparrow arguing with the sky, the paper landing on the stair, a wet towel on the wicker chair. The day arrives in pieces, small, a kettle's hum, a neighbour's call. Before the hurry and the heat, the world is kind, and has bare feet.

  • The Moon and I

    The moon walks home with me each night. I turn, it turns. I stop, it waits. It crosses fields and jumps the wires and sits above our gates. My mother says it stays up there for every child, in every town. I do not tell her she is wrong. It follows me. I've watched it. Down.

  • River

    The river never asks the way. It finds the low ground and it goes. It wears the stubborn rock to sand by coming back. That's all it knows. So when the wall in front is tall and nothing that I try gets through, I think of water, soft and small, and what a thousand tries can do.

  • Sparrow

    You used to nest above our fan, Brown, busy, never still. You stole the rice my grandma dried Upon the window sill. The fan is gone, the sill is glass, The rice comes in a pack. I put a bowl of water out. I hope that you come back.

  • Winter Sun

    On winter mornings, on the roof, The whole lane comes outside. The grandmas shell a hill of peas, The quilts are opened wide. Nobody asks the sun to come, Nobody pays a fee. It warms the rich man's balcony And our old roof, and me.

Where nature poems are used

  • World Environment Day (5 June), Earth Day (22 April) and Van Mahotsav week, at assembly or on the stage.
  • Plantation drives: one child reads "The Old Neem" or "पेड़" before the first sapling goes in.
  • Class magazines and wall newspapers, beside a drawing.
  • As a caption for a photograph of rain, a river or the moon.
  • Recitation competitions for junior and middle classes, where a nature poem is always a safe choice.

How to read a nature poem aloud

Nature poems are quiet, and the commonest mistake is to shout them. Let your voice follow the subject. Rain poems can patter quickly. A poem about a river should flow without stops at the line ends. A poem about the moon wants a slow, low voice. Before you recite, close your eyes and picture the thing itself, the actual tree near your house or the last rain you were caught in. The audience can hear the difference between a speaker who sees it and one who is only remembering words.

Write a verse of your own

Nature is the easiest subject for a first poem, because the material is outside the window. Try this: look at one thing for two full minutes, such as a crow, a cloud or a puddle. Write down three things you saw that you did not expect. Turn each into a line. You now have three lines that nobody else has written. Our guide How to write a poem takes you through the rest.

Pair the poem with an action

On Environment Day a poem is remembered longer when something follows it: a sapling planted, a bird bath filled, the classroom lights switched off for the period. Announce the action as the last line of your recitation. Slogans for the banner are on our sister site, Slogans.

Good to know

  • For Environment Day, pair a poem with one action the class will take. A poem is remembered longer when something is planted after it.
