Cleanliness and Swachhta slogans

For cleanliness drives, school campaigns, society notice boards and the wall that people keep littering near.

English

  • Clean street, proud street.

  • Use the bin. It has been waiting all day.

  • Your city is your home with a bigger door.

  • Litter is small. The habit is the problem.

  • Do not ask who will clean it. Ask who dirtied it.

  • Cleanliness is not a drive. It is a daily habit.

  • A clean school is a happy school.

  • If you can carry it full, you can carry it empty.

  • Spit and litter spoil a city faster than time does.

  • Clean hands, clean lanes, clean conscience.

  • Our lane, our pride.

  • The dustbin is ten steps away. Take them.

  • Leave the place better than you found it.

  • Sweepers clean up after us. Let us give them less to do.

  • A clean wall is the best painting.

  • स्वच्छता अपनाओ, बीमारी भगाओ।

Nothing matches. Try a shorter word, or choose All.

Where these slogans are needed

At the corner where litter collects, on the school corridor, outside the washrooms, on the society gate, at bus stands and markets, and on the cleanliness drive banner. Notice which of these are permanent. A drive lasts a morning, but a well-placed line beside a dustbin works every day.

Write your own

The best cleanliness slogans give people something to be proud of. "Our lane, our pride" works better than "Do not throw garbage here", because the first invites and the second accuses. Use "we" and "our". Name the place: our school, our mohalla, our park, our Ludhiana. A little humour helps too, as long as nobody is the target.

  • English pairs: clean and green, bin and win, litter and bitter, sweep and keep, tidy and mighty.
  • Hindi pairs: सफ़ाई and भलाई, कूड़ा and पूरा, स्वच्छता and सभ्यता, गंदगी and ज़िंदगी, शान and पहचान.
  • Punjabi pairs: ਸਫ਼ਾਈ and ਭਲਾਈ, ਗੰਦ and ਬੰਦ, ਸ਼ਾਨ and ਪਛਾਣ.

After the drive

  • Put a bin exactly where the heap was. People litter where there is no bin.
  • Give one lane or corridor to each class or group, with its name on a board.
  • Thank the sanitation workers by name at the assembly. A slogan about respect for their work belongs on every cleanliness poster.

What to avoid

  • Insulting lines about "dirty people". They make enemies, not clean streets.
  • Pictures of gods on walls to stop littering. Many people find this disrespectful.

Using these slogans

  • Polite and funny works better on a wall than angry. People obey a smile faster than a threat.
  • Put a dustbin where the slogan is. A slogan without a bin is only half the job.
