New rhymes and poems for children

New rhymes for small children, written by us. Each is four to eight lines, with a strong beat, so a child can learn it in a day and recite it with actions.

English

  • The Lazy Cat

    The cat sat on the garden wall and did not chase the bird at all. "Too hot," she said, "to run today." The bird said, "Thank you," and flew away.

    ShareYourGroup

  • My School Bag

    My school bag has a book, a box, a bottle and a pair of socks, a pencil with a chewed-up end, and half a biscuit for my friend.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Uncle Sun

    Uncle Sun gets up at six. He combs his hair with golden sticks. He walks across the sky all day and goes to bed behind the hay.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Pitter Patter

    Pitter patter on the pane, here it comes, the rain, the rain! Frogs are singing, drains are full, what a lovely day for school! (Teacher says: no holiday.) Pitter patter anyway.

    ShareYourGroup

  • Five Fat Samosas

    Five fat samosas on a plate, Papa took one, he couldn't wait. Four fat samosas, crisp and hot, Didi took one, thanks a lot. Three fat samosas by the tea, Dadi took one, who took three? No more samosas! Look at me: crumbs on my chin. It was me.

    ShareYourGroup

  • The Yawning Poem

    The stars are out, the lights are low, the moon says, "Time to sleep, you know." My eyes say yes, my feet say no. One more story. Then I'll go.

    ShareYourGroup

  • The Ant

    An ant went walking up my arm. I said, "Dear ant, I mean no harm, but that's my elbow, not a hill." The ant said nothing. Climbing still.

Good to know

  • Add an action to every line: point, clap, jump, yawn. Children remember with their hands.
  • For a recitation competition in nursery or Class 1, eight lines are plenty.
